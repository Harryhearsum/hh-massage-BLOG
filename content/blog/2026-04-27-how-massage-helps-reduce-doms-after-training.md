You've smashed a session at the gym. Two days later you can barely walk down the stairs. That's DOMS, and it's one of the most common things I see clients complaining about when they come into the clinic. The problem is, most people just accept it as part of training and get on with it. But it doesn't have to be that way.

Delayed onset muscle soreness, or DOMS as everyone calls it, is that deep muscle soreness that kicks in 24 to 72 hours after you've done something your body isn't used to. It's not the pump you feel during or right after training. This is different. Your legs feel stiff, your shoulders are sore when you try to move them, and even simple tasks feel heavy. I see people from gyms across Rotherham and Sheffield coming in absolutely crippled by it, especially after they've upped their training intensity or tried something completely new.

Here's what's actually happening in your muscles. When you train hard, you create micro-tears in the muscle fibres. That's normal and it's how adaptation happens. But the inflammatory response that follows is what causes that soreness. Your body floods the area with inflammatory cells and fluid, which builds up in the muscle. It's protective, but it's also painful. The good news is that sports massage can genuinely help speed that process up and get you moving again faster than you'd expect.

**Why massage actually works on DOMS**

The way massage helps is more practical than you might think. When I work on a client with bad DOMS, I'm not trying to force the soreness away or break down scar tissue or any of that. What I'm doing is helping move fluid out of the affected area and promoting blood flow to the muscles. That accelerates the body's natural recovery process.

Think of it like this. When inflammation builds up, it restricts movement and creates that stiffness. The muscle fibres are all swollen and irritated. Massage helps drain that fluid back into the lymphatic system where it can be processed. At the same time, it improves circulation, so fresh oxygen and nutrients get to the damaged tissue faster. Your body can then get on with the repair work properly, rather than being stuck dealing with pooled fluid and poor mobility.

In my experience, the best time to get a massage for DOMS is around 24 to 48 hours after training, once the acute soreness has kicked in. Too soon and you might irritate things further. Too late and you're missing the window where it can really help. I see this a lot with clients who train hard mid-week and come in on Friday absolutely destroyed, looking for relief before the weekend. The difference in how they feel is usually noticeable within hours.

**What you should expect from treatment**

A post workout massage for DOMS is different from what you might get at a spa in Sheffield or elsewhere. I'm not trying to relax you, though that's a nice side effect. I'm being quite specific about the areas that took the hammering. If you've done heavy leg day, I'm working the quads, glutes and hamstrings thoroughly. If you've been rowing hard or done heavy pressing, your upper back and shoulders get the attention.

The pressure is firm but not painful. There's a difference between good working pressure and pain that makes you tense up. When you're tense, your muscles contract, which defeats the purpose. What I'm aiming for is that point where it feels intense but you can actually relax into it. Most clients feel noticeably looser and less sore by the time they leave the treatment room.

One thing I always tell clients is that you don't have to wait until DOMS is at its worst before getting treatment. If you're training regularly, getting a post workout massage every 7 to 10 days helps prevent DOMS from building up in the first place. A lot of people in Treeton and around South Yorkshire who train consistently find that regular [massage appointments](/treatments) keep them moving better and bouncing back faster between training days. It becomes part of your training plan, not just something you do when you're desperate.

**Beyond just the massage**

Massage helps, but it's not a magic fix. The other things matter too. Getting decent sleep, eating enough protein, staying hydrated and actually moving the day after training all play a part. I see clients who get a massage then sit on the sofa all evening wondering why they're still sore. Light movement like walking or easy stretching actually speeds recovery more than lying still. The massage preps your muscles for that movement to happen without so much pain.

If your DOMS is so severe that you can't function or you're struggling even days later, that's worth getting sorted properly. It usually means either you've trained way too hard relative to your fitness level, or there's something else going on. That's where getting properly assessed makes sense, and [understanding what's happening](/about) with your body is the first step toward fixing it.

The bottom line is this: DOMS is recoverable, and you don't have to just accept being sore and stiff for three days every time you train hard. If you're training regularly and finding DOMS is limiting your performance or your ability to get back to the gym, sports massage makes a real difference. The clients I work with in Rotherham who've made it part of their recovery routine come back stronger and stay in the gym consistently instead of taking extra days off just because they're too sore to move.
