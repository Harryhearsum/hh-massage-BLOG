A lot of people think a sports massage is just a sports massage, right? Same technique, same pressure, same outcome. That's where most people go wrong. The truth is, what you need before an event is completely different from what you need after one, and getting that backwards can actually work against you.

I see clients turn up the day before a half marathon or a football match wanting a deep, intensive massage to "loosen everything up". It sounds logical, but it's the opposite of what their body needs. On the flip side, I'll get someone in three days after an event still hobbling around, thinking they don't need treatment because they're not competing anymore. They're also making a mistake.

Let me break down what's actually happening in each scenario and why the approach matters so much.

## Before you compete: what pre-event massage is really for

When someone comes to me a day or two before a race or match, the goal isn't to fix anything that's broken. You've already done your training. What we're doing is getting your nervous system primed, your blood flow moving, and your muscles ready to fire properly. Think of it like warming up a car engine on a cold morning, not rebuilding the engine itself.

A pre-event massage is lighter and more stimulating than the deep, remedial work I do during regular treatment sessions. I'm using faster, more rhythmic strokes to increase circulation and wake up your muscles. The pressure is moderate, nothing that's going to leave you sore or fatigued. If I hammered your legs hard the day before your event, you'd feel it during your competition. Your muscles would be tired and your nervous system would be in recovery mode instead of performance mode.

The timing matters too. Ideally, you want this done one to three days before your event, not the night before. If you get a massage the night before and it's too intense, you'll wake up feeling heavy and potentially with some inflammation. Give your body at least a bit of time to settle. What I usually recommend is a lighter, quicker session that's more about activation and preparation than repair.

I work with runners from across South Yorkshire, and this is something runners especially struggle with. You've got someone training hard for the Sheffield Marathon or doing the parkrun circuit around Rotherham, and they think a deep sports massage the night before will help. In reality, you want something more gentle that gets your blood moving and your mind right. ## After you've finished: why post-event matters more than you think

Now, post-event massage is where things get interesting and this is where I see real results. Your event's done, you've performed, but your body is in a very different state. Your muscles have been broken down through effort, you've got metabolic waste building up in the tissues, and inflammation is starting to kick in. This is exactly when a good massage can make a genuine difference to your recovery.

The goal here is quite different. You're not preparing anymore, you're repairing and clearing. Post-event massage helps flush out the byproducts of hard exercise like lactate and other metabolic waste. It reduces inflammation, it improves blood flow to damaged tissue, and it helps you move better faster. Most importantly, it genuinely shortens your recovery time, and I mean that from experience working with clients across Rotherham and Sheffield who come back for treatment after competing.

Timing here is more forgiving but still important. Ideally, you want a massage within 48 hours of finishing your event, and sooner is often better. I've had clients come in the same evening or the next morning, and the difference in how they feel a week later compared to people who didn't get treatment is noticeable. They bounce back quicker, they have less lingering soreness, and they're back to normal training sooner.

The pressure and technique are different too. I'm working deeper into the muscle here because there's actual repair work to do. I'm not trying to tire you out, but I am working to shift inflammation and break up the tightness that builds up after intense effort. The strokes can be slower and more sustained. Your muscles can handle it now because you're not about to go and perform again.

## The practical difference

Here's what you actually need to remember. Before your event, think light and stimulating. After your event, think moderate to deep and restorative. Before is about getting ready. After is about bouncing back faster. If you're in Treeton or anywhere else in South Yorkshire and you've got a race or match coming up, we can work out what you need for your specific situation.

The reality is, most people would benefit from both. A pre-event session gets you ready to perform well. A post-event session helps you recover properly so you can train again without lingering damage. Do both and you're giving yourself a real advantage over people who just train hard and hope the soreness goes away on its own.
