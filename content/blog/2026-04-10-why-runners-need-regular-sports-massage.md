Most runners I see come to me either injured or on the verge of it. They've been pounding the pavements around Rotherham or out on the trails in South Yorkshire, gradually building their mileage, and then something starts to feel off. A niggle in the calf. Tightness across the hip. A twinge in the knee that wasn't there last week. By the time they book in, they're often worried they'll have to stop running altogether.

Here's the thing though: a lot of this is preventable. Regular sports massage isn't something you do once you're broken. It's something you do to stay unbroken. I see this distinction make a huge difference with the runners I treat.

When you run, you're creating micro-damage in your muscles every single time your foot hits the ground. That's completely normal and that's how you get stronger. Your body repairs that damage and comes back more resilient. But if you never give your muscles proper attention between runs, tightness and restriction builds up. That restriction changes how you move. Your biomechanics shift slightly. And that's when injuries happen.

The running community in Sheffield and around Rotherham tends to be pretty dedicated, which is great, but I also see people who treat running like they're invincible. They do their miles, they do their strength work, maybe they stretch a bit, and they assume that's enough. What I tell most of my clients is that's only half the equation. Your muscles need someone who actually understands running biomechanics to work through the specific tension patterns you develop from repetitive running movement.

So what actually happens in your muscles when you run

Your calf, your quads, your glutes, your hip flexors, your IT band, your piriformis, your lower back stabilisers, all of these are working overtime. Some muscles are contracting hard, others are lengthening under load, and some are just hanging on for stability. Over time, the ones doing the heavy lifting get tighter. The ones that should be helping you stabilise get weaker and overcompensate. Your stride changes gradually without you even noticing.

This is where sports massage comes in. Unlike a relaxation massage, what we do is directly target the muscular and fascial restrictions that running creates. We're looking for the knots, the adhesions, the areas where your muscle fibres have essentially glued themselves together from repeated stress. When I'm working on a runner, I'm not just trying to make you feel nice in that moment. I'm trying to restore proper muscle length and function so your body can move the way it's supposed to.

I see runners come in who've had the same injury twice, and often it's because they only dealt with the symptoms, not the underlying tightness and restriction that caused it in the first place. Once we sort out the muscular tension and get your movement patterns back on track, that injury tends to stay gone.

Why regular treatment works better than occasional

One of the mistakes people make is booking a sports massage once they're already in pain. That works, sure, but you're dealing with the problem after it's happened. What I recommend to most runners is regular treatment as part of your training schedule. Think of it like a maintenance plan.

If you're training seriously, I'd suggest coming in every two to four weeks depending on your volume. That way, we catch restriction before it becomes a problem. We keep your tissue quality good. Your muscles stay supple and your movement stays efficient. You're less likely to get injured, and when you do have a session, there's less acute stuff to fix and more time to spend on the deeper work that actually improves your function.

I've got clients who've been running for years and barely get injuries now because they've built this into their routine. They see it as part of their training, not an optional extra.

The recovery piece matters too. Running creates inflammation in your tissues, and that inflammation needs to be managed properly. Regular sports massage helps your body deal with that inflammation faster. You recover quicker between hard sessions. You're fresher for your next training block. Over a season, that adds up.
