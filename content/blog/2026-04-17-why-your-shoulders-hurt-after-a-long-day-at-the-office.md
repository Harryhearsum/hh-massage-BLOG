If you're sitting at a desk right now, I want you to notice where your shoulders are. Are they up by your ears? Yeah, that's what I see with most of my clients from offices across Sheffield and Rotherham. You've probably got tension building in your traps and upper back without even realising it's happening, and by the time you leave work your shoulders feel like concrete.

The thing is, this isn't just discomfort. It's a mechanical problem that builds up hour after hour, day after day. Once you understand what's actually going wrong, you can start to fix it.

Your body has a natural posture when you're relaxed and upright. Your shoulders sit back and down, your shoulder blades are flat against your ribcage, and your neck is stacked nicely on top of your spine. But the moment you sit at a desk and look at a screen, everything changes. Your head naturally drifts forward. Your shoulders start to round. Your upper back rounds to compensate. This posture is sometimes called "upper crossed syndrome", and I see it constantly in desk workers.

Here's what happens mechanically. When your head goes forward even just a few centimetres, the muscles in your neck and shoulders have to work harder to hold it there. Your trapezius, levator scapulae, and the rhomboids in your upper back are now under constant tension just to keep your head from falling off. They're being asked to do a job they're not designed to do for eight hours straight. The muscles get tight, the blood flow gets restricted, and lactic acid builds up. By 4pm you've got that deep, aching shoulder pain that makes you want to just collapse.

The problem gets worse because of what happens in your chest. When you're hunched over a keyboard, your pectoral muscles get shortened and tight. Tight pecs pull your shoulders forward even more, which means your back muscles have to work even harder. It becomes a vicious cycle. You're essentially locked into a posture that's fighting against you.

Most people think the answer is to just sit up straighter, and they're not wrong, but conscious posture correction only works for about twenty minutes before you drift back into the slouch. Your brain gets busy, you're focused on your work, and boom, you're rounded forward again without even noticing. I see this a lot with clients, especially when they've got a deadline or they're deep in concentration.

The other thing that makes desk shoulder pain worse is that you're barely moving. Your shoulders are designed to move in multiple directions and planes of motion. They want to go forward and back, up and down, and in rotation. At a desk you're not doing any of that. You're stuck in one position for hours. The joints get stiff, the muscles don't get the stimulus they need, and everything becomes tighter and more restricted.

So what can you actually do about it?

First, you need to move more during the day. I'm not talking about going to the gym, though that would help. I mean actually moving your shoulders. Every hour, stand up and do some shoulder rolls backward. Clasp your hands behind your back and gently stretch your chest. Reach your arms up and take a few deep breaths. These small things interrupt the pattern and remind your body that it can move.

Second, your desk setup matters more than you think. Your screen should be at eye level so you're not looking down. Your keyboard should be at a height where your elbows can sit at roughly ninety degrees. Your chair needs to support your lower back. If you're spending eight hours a day at your desk, you owe it to yourself to get this right. I've seen people get dramatic relief just from adjusting their setup properly.
