Most people assume that if their muscles are already sore, getting a massage will only make things worse. I hear this all the time in clinic. Someone will come in after a tough gym session or a long run, feeling tender and tight, and they'll say something like "should I really be getting this done now?" The short answer is yes, but it's worth understanding why, because the reality is a bit more nuanced than just "massage helps soreness."

Let me explain what's actually happening in your muscles when you're sore. That post-workout soreness you feel, usually kicking in 24 to 72 hours after exercise, is called Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness or DOMS. It's not actually damage in the way people think. It's mostly inflammation and fluid buildup in and around the muscle fibres, combined with your nervous system being a bit sensitised to the area. Your body is adapting to the new stress you've put it under. This is normal and it's part of how you get stronger.

Here's the thing though: soreness doesn't mean you shouldn't touch the area. In fact, it's often the perfect time to get some work done.

When you come to me with sore muscles, what I'm doing is helping your body manage that inflammation more efficiently. A proper sports massage increases blood flow to the area, helps shift some of that fluid that's making you stiff, and tells your nervous system that the area is safe. I'm not trying to beat the soreness out of you, which is a common misconception. I'm facilitating recovery. The pressure and movement we use in treatment helps your body's natural healing processes move along a bit faster than if you just sat at home stretching and icing.

I see this distinction matter a lot with clients here in Rotherham and across Sheffield who are training regularly. The runners I work with, the people doing CrossFit, the gym goers hitting new personal records, they all understand this: if you only get treatment when you feel good, you're missing the window where it actually helps most. When you're sore is exactly when your tissues are primed to respond to good hands-on work.

That said, there is a difference between useful soreness and actual injury. If you've genuinely done damage, torn something, or injured yourself during training, that's different. You'd know the difference. Real injury comes with sharp pain, swelling, bruising, instability, or pain that doesn't settle down. DOMS is sore but it's not painful in that acute way. It's more of an ache and stiffness that gets worse if you don't move.

The mistake most people make is overdoing it too soon after exercise. What I tell most of my clients is that soreness is a sign you've done something new or intense, not a sign to go harder the next day. You need recovery built into your training week. But massage isn't the same as training. Massage supports recovery. It's active recovery, if you like. Your body still gets the stimulus from the workout, but you're managing the aftermath better.

Another thing people get wrong is assuming that rest alone sorts soreness out. It does eventually, sure. But you'll heal faster and feel better sooner with appropriate soft tissue work. I've had clients come in a few days after a race or a heavy leg session, and after one good sports massage they've got their range of motion back and they're moving normally within a couple of days. Without it, they're still hobbling around a week later.

The type of massage matters though. A gentle Swedish spa massage might feel nice, but it's not going to do much for real muscular soreness. You need someone who understands how muscles work and how to actually address the issue. That's where sports massage comes in. It's more specific, it goes deeper, and it's designed to fix problems not just make you feel relaxed. I focus on the areas that are causing you problems, use the right pressure and techniques to get a real result, and give you advice on what to do between sessions.

Timing is also worth thinking about. If you're sore today, waiting three or four days before you get a massage is fine. Your body will benefit from it. You don't need to jump on it immediately. In fact, waiting a day or two means you've got a clearer picture of what's actually sore and where the tension is building up. Your body's also had time to start its own healing process, so when you do get treatment, it slots in perfectly with that.

If you're training regularly and you're always just dealing with soreness on your own, you're making your recovery harder than it needs to be. Whether you're training for something specific or just hitting the gym a few times a week, getting [sports massage treatment](/treatments) when you're sore actually helps you come back stronger for the next session. It's not weakness or babying yourself. It's smart recovery.

I've worked with plenty of people across South Yorkshire who've never had a proper sports massage before, and they're always surprised at how much it changes how they feel. They go from hobbling around for days to moving normally, and they get back to training properly sooner. That's not magic, that's just physiology. You're giving your body the right conditions to recover.
