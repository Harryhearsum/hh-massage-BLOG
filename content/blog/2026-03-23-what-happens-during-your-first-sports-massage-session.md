# What Happens During Your First Sports Massage Session

If you've never had a sports massage before, it's natural to wonder what you're walking into. Will it hurt? How long will it take? What do I need to do? I get these questions all the time from clients across Rotherham and Sheffield, and I'm going to walk you through exactly what your first session looks like.

The truth is, there's nothing to be nervous about. Your first appointment is as much about me understanding *you* as it is about providing treatment. It's a conversation, an assessment, and the start of getting you feeling better.

## The Pre-Appointment Chat

When you arrive, we'll spend the first 10–15 minutes talking. This isn't filler time—it's crucial. I'll ask you about:

- **What brought you in.** Are you dealing with a specific injury? Training for something? Sitting at a desk all day with tight shoulders?
- **Your medical history.** Past injuries, surgeries, ongoing conditions, medications—anything that might affect how I treat you.
- **Your current symptoms.** Where does it hurt? When does it hurt? What makes it better or worse?
- **Your lifestyle.** Are you an athlete, a weekend runner, an office worker? I see a lot of different people—from footballers in Sheffield to builders in Rotherham to people who spend eight hours a day hunched over a keyboard.
- **Your goals.** Do you want to get back to sport? Reduce pain? Improve mobility? Prevent future problems?

**This is where most people go wrong**: they're vague about what they need. The more specific you can be, the better I can help. Tell me if you've got a match on Saturday, or if you're just tired of your neck aching by Friday afternoon.

## The Physical Assessment

Once I've got the full picture, I'll do a physical assessment. This involves:

- **Observing your posture and movement.** Often the real problem isn't where you think it is. I might notice that your lower back pain is actually stemming from tight hip flexors, or that your shoulder issue is rooted in poor thoracic mobility.
- **Palpation.** I'll feel the affected areas and surrounding tissues to identify where tension, restriction, or dysfunction is living.
- **Specific movement tests.** Depending on your issue, I might ask you to move in certain ways—bend forward, rotate, reach overhead—to understand your range of motion and where you're limited.

This assessment takes 10–15 minutes and it's completely painless. It tells me exactly what I'm working with.

## The Treatment Itself

Now the actual massage begins. Here's what you need to know:

### Pressure and Communication

Sports massage isn't a relaxing spa experience. It's therapeutic and often quite firm. **But "firm" doesn't mean "painful."** There's a difference between good pressure that's doing its job and pain that means something's wrong.

I always tell clients: if something hurts in a sharp or pinching way, tell me immediately. If it's a deep, intense pressure that's slightly uncomfortable but not sharp—that's usually the right zone. On a scale of 1–10, you're probably sitting at a 6 or 7 most of the time.

**Talk to me.** If the pressure needs adjusting, let me know. If you need a break, say so. This is a collaboration.

### What You'll Feel

Depending on your issue, I might use:

- **Deep tissue techniques** to break down tension and adhesions in the muscle.
- **Soft tissue release** to free up restricted fascia.
- **Stretching and mobilisation** to improve your range of motion.
- **Trigger point work** to release particularly stubborn knots.

You'll probably feel some immediate relief as we work, but you might also feel some discomfort in areas that are really tight. That's normal.

## Duration and What Happens After

A first appointment typically takes **45–60 minutes total**—that includes the consultation, assessment, and treatment. Subsequent visits might be shorter, depending on what we're working on.

**After the session**, you might feel:

- Immediate relief and looseness
- A bit sore or tender the next day (like gentle muscle soreness after exercise)
- Significantly more mobile
- Tired—this is your body relaxing and recovering

I'll usually recommend not doing intense exercise for 24 hours after treatment, and I'll give you advice on what to do at home—stretches, ice or heat, posture adjustments. Sometimes the work we do here needs to be supported by what you do between sessions.

## What You Should Bring

- Any medical notes or scan results if you've got them
- A list of medications if relevant
- Comfortable clothes you can change out of
- An open mind and honest feedback
